Team SpaceX Hackers1 / 7

Crews rehearse on Earth before they go. Which Earth places actually look like where they are going?

  • Artemis III targets the lunar south pole: steep crater rims, long shadows from a sun that circles the horizon, ice-stable cold traps.
  • Today's analog sites (Hawaii, Iceland, the Dry Valleys) were picked by experience, not by measuring them against a specific landing region.
  • We asked a measurable question: for each candidate Moon region and Mars landing site, which 20 to 30 km blocks of Earth have the closest terrain, lighting, thermal and surface statistics, and how far off are they?

a ranked, checkable list instead of a hunch

Problem2 / 7

Same code on both worlds: fingerprint the target, then score every Earth block.

1. Moon targets
LRO LOLA 40 m elevation + LRO Diviner temperature and ice stability for 9 Artemis III regions.
2. Earth blocks
Copernicus GLO-30 terrain, MODIS temperature, snow, NDVI, NASA POWER climate for 12,362 blocks.
3. Score
10 categories: slopes, multi-scale roughness, craters, landing area, low-sun shadows, thermal, ice, geology.
4. Novelty
Keep only places at least 150 km from 166 catalogued analog sites (251 for Mars).
5. Verify
Landsat 8/9 and Sentinel-2 check that the surface is really bare rock.

Mars: 68,496 arid blocks at 200 m vs Jezero, Gale, Meridiani and Oxia Planum (HRSC+MOLA terrain, MGS TES albedo and thermal inertia). Validation: the score separates known analogs from forests and farmland with AUC 0.82.

What we found, and where it falls short.

Moon, new

Ngari, western Tibet

Closest to Slater Plain. Stays in the top 40 in 87 % of 1,000 re-weightings; 82 % bare ground; 243 km from the nearest known analog.

Moon, new

Pamir, near Muztagh Ata

Closest to Mons Mouton Plateau. Stability 87 %; 514 km from the nearest known site; 22 to 33 % sparse vegetation.

Moon, conditional

Southern Tibet, Tingri

Steep match for Malapert Massif; ranking is weight-sensitive and one corner has a glacier.

Mars, new

Chagai / Taftan, Pakistan-Iran

Near-identical to Oxia Planum in slope, relief and albedo (0.20 vs 0.195); 100 % bare; 392 km from known sites.

Mars, new

Beishan Gobi, Gansu

Second Oxia match: stability 99 %, 100 % bare, 339 km from the nearest catalogued site.

Every site

Gaps we state in numbers

No Earth block closes the temperature gap (about 275 K vs 105 to 153 K) or the lighting gap (0 vs 365 circling-sun days). Mars air pressure is about 150 times lower.

Place names are approximate; "new" means no analog program found within 150 km in catalogues and web searches. Prior art exists, so we claim a quantified match, not a first.

Results4 / 7

Terralog turns the research into something anyone can explore.

  • Glowing globe: every scored block, coloured live by how well it matches.
  • Pick Moon or Mars; cards show each site's match score, why it matches, and what it cannot reproduce.
  • Move sliders (temperature, rain, slope, ice) or type "cold, dry, flat plain": the ranking updates instantly across all 12,362 blocks.
  • Enter a site and walk it at eye height in 3D.
The app5 / 7

Twin Terrain Lab: real Tibet next to the real Moon, at the same scale.

Both blocks are real elevation data at 40 m per pixel (Copernicus GLO-30 vs LRO LOLA). Slopes overlap by 61 %. Drop the sun to the Moon's 1.5° polar angle and the lighting gap becomes visible. Morph one into the other; the same view works for Mars sites at 200 m.

Why it matters, and what is next.

  • Impact: mission planners and analog programs get a reproducible, data-backed shortlist of training and test sites matched to specific landing regions, with the gaps spelled out.
  • Open: the full pipeline, data and app are public; anyone can re-run it with new targets.
  • Next: field-check the sites, add thermal and mineralogy data, extend Mars validation, and add more Artemis and Mars candidate regions.
